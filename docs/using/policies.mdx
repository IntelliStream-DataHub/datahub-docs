# Policies

  <Persona>Compliance</Persona>

<KeyIdea>
A **policy** is a rule attached to a data set: how long its data is kept, who may access it
and on what terms, what requirements it must meet.

Today a policy is a <span className="hl">declarative record</span>, it documents the rule
where the data lives. Enforcement, the platform actually acting on it, is the roadmap part,
and it is one of the
[two building blocks still being built](/start/core-ideas#policies-the-rules).
</KeyIdea>

<Roadmap>
**Policies are recorded, not enforced.** Nothing in the platform currently expires data,
gates access or checks requirements because a policy says so: retention is a
[coming feature](/administration/data-lifecycle#lever-2--retention) planned together with
policy enforcement, and access today comes entirely from
[roles in your identity provider](/administration/dataset-permissions). Treat what you write
as documented intent, and write it as though it will be enforced, because it will be.
</Roadmap>

## Why record rules before they are enforced

It sounds backwards, and it is the right order, for the same reason the
[model](/concepts/ontology) comes before the analytics, and for the same reason
[governance works best attached to the data it governs](/concepts/data-governance).

Most organisations keep data rules in procedure documents: a retention schedule in a PDF, an
access principle in a policy manual, a regulatory requirement in somebody's head. The rule
and the data it governs live in different places, so the rule is invisible exactly where
decisions get made.

A recorded policy fixes the visibility problem on its own:

- **The rule is findable from the data.** Whoever opens the data set sees what governs it,
  rather than needing to know which document to check.
- **Intent survives people.** "We keep this for ten years because of obligation X" stops
  being tribal knowledge, the same argument as for the
  [model itself](/value/cost-of-doing-nothing#2--knowledge-concentrated-in-people).
- **Enforcement becomes a switch, not a project.** When it ships, the rules are already
  written, reviewed and attached. <span className="hl">Organisations that waited will be
  starting their retention workshops while yours activates.</span>

## The three kinds

| Kind | What it records | Enforced, once shipped |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Lifecycle** | How long data is kept, and what happens when the clock runs out | Measurement history actually expires on schedule |
| **Access** | Who may read or write, and on what terms | Gating, aligned with the [organization groups](/administration/identity-providers#organization-groups-will-carry-data-set-access) work |
| **Requirement** | What the data set must satisfy, completeness, standards, review cadence | Checks that flag when the requirement is not met |

## Writing a policy worth having

The test for a good policy is the same as for a good
[description](/using/datasets#creating-one): a colleague in a year should understand not
just the rule but the reason.

<Steps>
  <Step title="Name the obligation, not just the number">
    "Retain 10 years, [NORSOK](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NORSOK_standard) requirement
    for well data" beats "retain 10 years". The reason is what lets a successor revisit the
    rule safely when the obligation changes.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Attach it to the right boundary">
    Policies live on [data sets](/using/datasets), so the data set boundary decides what a
    rule can govern. If one rule must cover two data sets, or half of one, that is a sign
    the boundaries need a look first.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Keep lifecycle policies aligned with your audit horizon">
    <span className="t-accent">A reported figure can only ever be traced to inputs that
    still exist</span>, so [retention](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Data_retention) rules
    on anything feeding a regulated report should outlive the report's auditability.
    [Retention versus traceability →](/administration/data-lifecycle#retention-versus-traceability)
  </Step>
  <Step title="Review them like access, on a cadence">
    A quarterly pass over the policies alongside the
    [permission review](/administration/dataset-permissions#designing-a-permission-scheme),
    so recorded intent tracks reality.
  </Step>
</Steps>
