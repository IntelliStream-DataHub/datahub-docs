# Flows

<Personas>
  <Persona primary>Engineers</Persona>
  <Persona primary>Operations</Persona>
  <Persona>Domain experts</Persona>
</Personas>

<KeyIdea>
A **flow** is a small pipeline that DataHub runs for you. It reads data from DataHub,
changes it and writes the result back, <span className="hl">on a schedule, when an event is
recorded, or when you start it</span>. Each step is a **processor**, such as *Read
timeseries*, *Clamp* or *Write events*, and you join the steps into a tree in the console's
**Flows** section, with no code.

Every save is a new **version**. A flow runs only once it is **deployed**, and it then acts
as you or as a registered client, with that identity's access and no more.

This page is the how-to, task by task. For the story of what a run does and why flows behave
as they do, read [How flows work](/concepts/how-flows-work) first.
</KeyIdea>

## An example

A flow meter sometimes reports spikes far beyond its range. A flow that cleans them up
every hour has three steps:

| Step | Processor | What it does |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | **Read timeseries** | Reads `pump-12.flow` for the hour just ended |
| 2 | **Clamp** | Lowers every value above 250 to 250 |
| 3 | **Write timeseries** | Writes the result to `pump-12.flow_clamped`, created when the flow is saved |

The same flow written out as JSON, field by field, is the example on the
[flow definition reference](/using/flow-definition#a-complete-example).

## Who sees the section

**Flows** appears in the console header when your organisation has flows turned on, the
`flow` setting whoever operates the platform sets for it. Without it the entry is missing
and the section's pages say *This feature is not enabled for your organization.* Beyond
that, what you see and may do is decided per flow, by two organisation groups and the
flow's data set:

| To | You need |
| --- | --- |
| See a flow, its versions and its runs | `/flows/read` and read access to its data set |
| Create, change, preview, deploy, run, cancel, switch off or delete it | `/flows/write` and write access to its data set |

`DATAHUB_ADMIN` may do everything. A flow you may see but not change opens with *You can
see this flow but not change it.* A flow whose data set was deleted is shown only to people
with write access to every data set, so they can move it to another data set or delete it.
[Enabling flows →](/administration/flow-service#setting-it-up)

## The section

| Tab | What it is for |
| --- | --- |
| **Flows** | The list of flows, and **New flow** |
| **Runs** | How every deployed flow is doing, at a glance. See [the Runs tab](#the-runs-tab) |
| **Failed runs** | Runs that failed after their last attempt and that nobody has dealt with yet. See [Failed runs](#failed-runs) |
| **Clients** | The registered clients a flow can run as. [Setting one up →](/administration/flow-credentials#registered-clients) |

The count on the **Flows** entry in the header is the number of open failed runs.

**The list** shows the flows in data sets you can read, with their **Name**, **Dataset**,
**Version**, **Deployment** (*not deployed*, *version 3 deployed and on*, *version 3
deployed, switched off*) and when they were **Updated**. Each row has the button that fits:
**Deploy**, **Deploy version 4** when a newer version has been saved than the one running,
or **Switch off** and **Switch on**. Click a row to open the flow. Agents are not listed
here; they have [a section of their own](/using/agents).

## Building a flow

<Steps>
  <Step title="Name it and pick its data set">
    **New flow** opens an empty editor with a **Name** and a **Dataset** above it. The
    external id follows from the name, shown as *Saved as pump_12_flow_clamped*, and cannot
    change once saved. The data set decides who may see and change the flow, and only data
    sets you can write are offered.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Add the steps">
    **Add processor** opens a searchable list, grouped as **Read and write**, **Transform**,
    **Detect** and **Agent**. Pick one and fill in its form. Click a processor in the tree
    to open its form again.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Connect them">
    In the toolbar, choose **From…**, an **Outcome…** and **To…**, then **Connect**. Every
    outcome must be connected or ended; see [outcomes](#outcomes-and-connections).
  </Step>
  <Step title="Set when it runs">
    **Flow settings** holds the name, description, data set, **Trigger** and, under
    **Advanced**, the **Delay** and **Limits**. A new flow starts on a schedule of every
    hour. See [when a flow runs](#when-a-flow-runs).
  </Step>
  <Step title="Save, preview, deploy">
    **Save** checks the flow and stores it as a new version. **Preview** tries it without
    writing anything. **Deploy** puts it to work.
  </Step>
</Steps>

The editor has four tabs:

| Tab | What it shows |
| --- | --- |
| **Processing tree** | The steps and connections as a diagram, with the toolbar above. Solid arrows are connections; dashed lines are outcomes that end there. **Remove selected** removes the processor or connection you clicked |
| **JSON** | The whole flow as JSON, the same document the tree is drawn from. Edit it, press **Apply JSON**, then **Save**. Some settings are only here, such as parameters, a schedule's time zone and how many runs may run at once: [the full list](/using/flow-definition) |
| **Versions** | Every saved version, who saved it and when |
| **Runs** | This flow's runs, newest first. Previews are left out unless you tick **Show previews** |

A processor's form shows what it needs; settings that have a sensible default are folded
under **Advanced**. A field can often take a placeholder such as
`${parameters.maxFlow}` instead of a fixed value, filled in when the flow runs:
[References in flows](/using/flow-references).

## The processors

| Processor | Group | In plain words |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Read timeseries** | Read and write | Reads one numeric series over the run's time window, optionally with some history before it (**Lookback**) |
| **Read events** | Read and write | Reads the events in the run's window, of the types, subtypes and statuses you name |
| **Triggering events** | Read and write | Reads the events that woke the run, for a flow whose trigger is **Events** |
| **Clamp** | Transform | Limits values to a range: below **Min** becomes Min, above **Max** becomes Max. Every point is kept |
| **Threshold periods** | Detect | Finds the periods a series spends above, or below, a threshold, as events with a start and an end |
| **Agent** | Agent | A model with tools, acting on the events given to it. Usually built in [Agents](/using/agents) instead |
| **Write timeseries** | Read and write | Writes the points to a series. **New series** creates the target when the flow is saved; **Existing series** writes to one that is there already |
| **Write events** | Read and write | Writes one event per row it is given, such as each period Threshold periods found |

A run never creates a series on its own: the person saving the flow does, and the save
lists them, *Created series pump-12.flow_clamped*. Every property, default and rule is on the
[flow definition reference](/using/flow-definition#the-processors).

## Outcomes and connections

Each processor sends its results out through named **outcomes**, such as `success` or, for
Threshold periods, `alarms`. In the JSON they are called relationships. Every outcome must
go somewhere:

- **Connect it** to the next step. One outcome may feed several steps.
- **End it there**: tick it under **End these outcomes here** in the processor's form. What
  is sent to it stops.
- Some outcomes **end by themselves**, such as a writer's `success`, and some **fail the
  run** if anything reaches them, such as a writer's `failure`.

An outcome that is a step's whole result, such as Threshold periods' `alarms`, cannot be
ended and must be connected. Connections may not loop back to an earlier step.

**Retry a failing step**, under **Advanced** on the read and write processors, tries that one
step again with the same input, up to 10 attempts with a wait of at most one minute between
them, before the run counts as failed.

## Saving, versions and problems

**Save** sends the whole flow to be checked and, if all is well, stores it as the next
version: *Saved as version 4.* A saved version never changes.

When something is wrong, nothing is saved and every problem is listed at once above the tabs,
*The flow has 3 problems:*, each with where it was found, such as
*Clamp › Max* or *Flow settings › Cron (six fields)*. Click a problem to open the form where
it can be fixed. Processors with a problem are outlined in red in the tree, and the field is
marked in its form. Common ones:

| Problem | Fix |
| --- | --- |
| *Its "success" outcome goes nowhere* | Connect it, or end it there |
| *"alarms" cannot be ended there* | That outcome is the step's result: connect it to a writer |
| *The connections form a cycle* | Remove the connection that leads back |
| *Required for a schedule: a six-field cron expression* | Pick a schedule, or fix the custom one |
| *A scheduled flow's parameters need defaults* | Give every parameter a `default` in the JSON tab |

If someone else saved the flow while you had it open, your save is refused with *Someone
saved this flow after you opened it.* Reload to see their version, then make your change
again.

**Versions**, then **Open**, loads an older version into the editor; *Save to make it the
current version.* Saving a new version does not change what is deployed: the running version
stays until you deploy the new one.

## Preview, the dry run

**Preview** runs the flow now, **as you**, over **the last hour**, and writes nothing. It
runs the saved version, or what is in the editor if you have unsaved changes, so you can try
a change before saving it. The run page opens and follows it live.

- Readers really read, with your access, so the steps see real data.
- Writers report what they would have written, and no series is created.
- A preview never counts as a failed run, never moves a schedule and cannot be re-run;
  preview again instead.
- It needs about a minute left on your sign-in. *Your sign-in is about to expire. Reload
  the page and try again.* means reload and press Preview again.

## Deploying

A saved flow does nothing until it is deployed. Deploying chooses which version runs and
**whose identity** its runs act as. A run can only read and write what that identity may,
and its failure events are written as that identity, so it needs write access to the flow's
data set.

| Button | Runs act as |
| --- | --- |
| **Deploy** | **You.** It takes you through your sign-in page, where you grant the flow a long-lived token, and back: *Deployed. Its runs act as you.* Your account needs the `offline_access` role |
| **Run as a client** | **A registered client** you pick from the list, such as `reporting`. You can only choose a client whose groups you all hold. [Setting one up →](/administration/flow-credentials#registered-clients) |

Once deployed, the line under the flow's name says so, for example *Version 4 · version 4
deployed and on · runs as jane · next run 14:00*. **Deploy again** deploys the current version
as you; **Deploy version 4** in the list moves the deployment to the latest version and
keeps whoever it runs as. A run already under way finishes on the version it started with.

**A deployment as you does not last for ever.** It runs on your sign-in session for the
flow, which each run renews; with the sign-in server's defaults it ends after **30 days
without a run**, and at once if your account is disabled or your offline access revoked.
The flow then shows *needs re-authorisation: deploy it again* and stops: deploy it again to
resume. A run always has your access as it is at the time, so losing a data set breaks the
flow without ending the session. A flow that must keep running whoever built it should run as
a client. [How long a deployment lasts →](/administration/agents#how-long-a-deployment-lasts)

## When a flow runs

Every run covers a **time window**, and the readers read only what falls inside it. The
**Trigger** under **Flow settings** decides when runs start and what window each one gets:

| Trigger | Runs start | Each run covers |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Manual** | Only when someone presses **Run now** | The **Window** you set, up to now: `1h` covers the hour before the run starts |
| **Schedule** | By itself, at the times the schedule sets | The time since the tick before, so every moment is covered once |
| **Events** | When an event it **Listens for** is recorded | The time since the run before |

**Schedules** on offer are *Every 5 minutes*, *Every 15 minutes*, *Every hour*, *Every day
at midnight*, *Every Monday at midnight*, or **Custom (cron)**: a
[cron expression](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cron) of six fields, seconds first, so
`0 30 6 * * *` is 06:30 every day. **Delay**, under **Advanced**, starts each run that long
after its tick, so readings that arrive late are in; durations are written as `30s`, `15m`,
`1h 30m` or `2d`.

**Time zone.** A schedule is read in UTC unless the flow says otherwise. The form has no
field for it: in the **JSON** tab, add a `timezone` to the trigger, such as
`"timezone": "Europe/Oslo"`, and the ticks then follow that clock through daylight saving.
Times in the console are shown in your own local time.

**Events.** The trigger listens for events by **External ids**, **Event types**,
**Subtypes**, **Statuses**, **Sources** and **Metadata**, and **Leave out sources** skips
some, such as `agent:*`. A field left empty matches anything. Only events in a data set the
flow's identity may read wake it, and a burst of events makes one run. The event only wakes
the flow; to have the flow read those events, press **Add the triggering events to the
flow** and connect the **Triggering events** step it adds.

**Run now** appears once the flow is deployed, switched on and has no unsaved changes. It runs
the deployed version: on a schedule, over the latest tick's window, without moving the
schedule; otherwise over the **Window**. A flow without one cannot be started this way.

**Missed ticks.** Ticks that fell due while the flow service was down are run when it is back,
oldest first. Ticks missed while a flow was switched off, not yet deployed, or waiting for
re-authorisation are not.

## When a run fails

A run that fails is tried again by itself, **2 attempts** in all by default, after a short
wait that grows with each attempt. A run that goes past its time limit, **15 minutes** by
default and at most an hour, fails. Both are under **Flow settings**, **Advanced**, **Limits**.

Two failures are not retried: a deployment that needs re-authorisation, since no attempt
would get further, and a flow whose data set was deleted, which the service switches off
(*switched off: …*) rather than fail.

When the last attempt fails, the run lands under **Failed runs**, and an event of type `flow`
and subtype `run-failed`, status `open`, is written in the flow's data set, so failures are
in the [event log](/using/events) beside everything else.

### Failed runs

The list is grouped by flow, then by error code, with each failed run's **Window**, when it
**Failed**, the **Error** and the **Attempt**. Each row has:

| Button | What it does |
| --- | --- |
| **View run** | Opens the run, to see what went wrong |
| **Re-run** | Runs the same window again, with the same parameter values, on the version deployed now. When it succeeds, the failure is closed and leaves the list by itself |
| **Dismiss** | Closes the failure without running anything: *The window stays unprocessed.* Needs write access to the flow's data set |

**Re-run all** and **Dismiss all** do the same for every failure listed. A later successful
run of the same window also closes it. Re-running needs the flow to be deployed and on, and
re-running the same failure twice starts it only once.

## The Runs tab

One row per deployed flow you may see, with what it has **Running** and **Queued**, its
**Last run** and, folded away for the fine ones, its **Next run**. A summary line counts
them, and the page refreshes itself every few seconds while anything is running or queued.

| State | When |
| --- | --- |
| **Problem** | The latest run failed, the deployment needs re-authorisation, or the service switched the flow off |
| **Watch** | A run is being retried, runs have waited over 5 minutes to start, or a scheduled run is overdue |
| **Fine** | Nothing to report |
| **Switched off** | Someone switched it off |

Flows with a problem or something to watch come first, under **Problems and flows to
watch**; when there are none, *Everything looks fine.*

## The run page

Opened from a flow's **Runs** tab, from **Failed runs** or by **Preview**. At the top, the
run's status, **Pending**, **Running**, **Succeeded**, **Failed** or **Cancelled**, and its
facts: **Version**, **Attempt** (such as 1/2), **Trigger**, **Runs as**, **Window**,
**Started**, **Finished**, the **Error** if it failed, and the **Parameters** it ran with.
While the run is going the page follows it live.

**What happened** lists every record of the run: each time a step read, wrote, created,
changed, routed or ended a piece of data. Columns:

| Column | Shows |
| --- | --- |
| **Time**, **Attempt**, **Processor** | When, in which attempt, and by which step |
| **Action** | What the step did: **Read**, **Write**, **Create**, **Modify content**, **Route**, **End** and so on |
| **Outcome** | The outcome it was sent to |
| **Item** | Which piece of data, numbered and named after its series, such as *#2 · pump-12.flow* |
| **Data** | How much it held, such as *3,600 points* or *12 events* |
| **Source / destination** | The series or events read or written, and over which times |
| **Changed attributes** | What the step noted on the item, such as `clamp.count=4` |

Pick a step in **All processors** to see only its records, or click a record to follow its
item: *Only item #2, what it came from and what it became.* **Show all** goes back. A run
keeps at most 100,000 records per attempt; past that the list ends with a *truncated* row.
Records are kept with the run's history, 90 days by default.

| Button | When | What it does |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Cancel** | While the run is pending or running | A pending run is cancelled at once; a running one is stopped shortly after. The window is left uncovered, and a cancelled run is not retried and is not a failed run |
| **Re-run** | Once it has ended, except a preview | A new run over the same window and values, on the version deployed now |

## Switching off and deleting

**Switch off**, on the flow or in the list, pauses it: no new runs start, runs still
waiting are cancelled, and ticks missed while it is off are not run later. **Switch on**
resumes it from the next tick. Before planned maintenance on the flow service, switch off the
flows whose missed runs you do not want made up afterwards.

**Delete** removes the flow, its versions and its runs. Series it wrote, and the
`run-failed` events, stay.

## Flows and agents

An [agent](/using/agents) is a flow whose middle step is the **Agent** processor: a model
with tools, woken by events, ending by saying whether a person needs to look. It is built in
the **Agents** section, with a form of its own, and listed there rather than under Flows. The
rest is shared: versions, preview, deploying as you or as a client, the run page, and failed
runs, which appear under **Failed runs** here like any other. **Open in Flows** on an agent
shows it in this editor.
