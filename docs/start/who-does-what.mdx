# Who does what

<Personas>
  <Persona primary>Leadership</Persona>
  <Persona primary>Project sponsors</Persona>
  <Persona>Administrators</Persona>
</Personas>

<KeyIdea>
A DataHub rollout needs five roles. Only one of them is technical.

The role that matters most, and is most often assigned wrongly, is the **domain expert**
who says what the things in your operation actually are. That cannot be outsourced to a
supplier or a data team, because it is the part only your own people know.
</KeyIdea>

## The five roles

| Role | Accountable for | Typically |
| --- | --- | --- |
| <span className="t-brand">Sponsor</span> | Choosing the first question; unblocking access to source systems | Operations director, plant manager, COO |
| <span className="t-brand">Domain expert</span> | What the things *are* and how they relate, the model itself | Process engineer, maintenance lead, senior operator |
| <span className="t-brand">Data steward</span> | Naming conventions, data set boundaries, model quality over time | Engineering or asset-information lead |
| <span className="t-brand">Administrator</span> | Users, permissions, retention, cost, uptime | IT / OT operations |
| <span className="t-brand">Integrator</span> | Getting data out of source systems and into DataHub | Automation engineer, systems integrator, or a developer |

Small organisations routinely combine these, one person can be steward and administrator,
and the sponsor is often also the domain expert. What does not work is having *none* of
them, particularly the domain expert.

## The mistake worth avoiding

The most common failure pattern in this category is this: the platform is bought by IT,
the model is built by a supplier or an internal data team, and the engineers who actually
know the plant are consulted at the review meeting.

The result is a model that is **technically correct and operationally useless**. It uses the
categories a data modeller would choose rather than the ones the plant uses, so the people
who were supposed to benefit look at it and do not recognise their own operation.

<span className="hl">The model must be built by people who know the operation, using words the
operation already uses.</span> The platform's job, and DataHub's specific design goal, is
to make that possible without those people needing to write code.

## What each role does, week by week

### Weeks 1–2 · Frame it

- **Sponsor** picks one question the organisation genuinely wants answered, and names the
  people who will answer it. [Choosing a first question →](/value/where-to-start)
- **Administrator** stands up the platform (or gets it stood up) and creates accounts.
  [Installation →](/administration/installing) ·
  [Identity providers →](/administration/identity-providers)
- **Integrator** confirms which source systems can be read, and how.

### Weeks 3–6 · Model and land data

- **Domain expert** runs the modelling sessions: what exists, what it is a kind of, how it
  connects. [Building your model →](/concepts/building-your-model)
- **Data steward** sets the naming conventions before anything is created at volume, and
  decides the data set boundaries. [Naming and standards →](/concepts/naming-and-standards) ·
  [What governance asks of a steward →](/concepts/data-governance)
- **Integrator** lands the first two or three sources as time series and events.
- **Administrator** grants read and write on the right data sets.
  [Permissions →](/administration/dataset-permissions)

### Weeks 7–12 · Answer the question, then widen

- **Domain expert** and analysts answer the original question against the model, and
  document how.
- **Sponsor** reviews the answer, and decides the second question.
- **Data steward** captures what the first pass got wrong about the model and fixes it,
  this always happens and is a sign of a healthy project, not a failed one.
  [Grown, not built →](/concepts/digital-twin#grown-not-built)
- **Administrator** sets retention and reviews cost.
  [Data lifecycle →](/administration/data-lifecycle)

## How much time this actually takes

Realistic expectations, for a first project scoped to one site or one process area:

<Stats>
  <Stat value="1–2 days" label="Domain expert, modelling" note="Spread over two or three sessions, not one marathon" />
  <Stat value="~1 day" label="Administrator, setup" note="More if identity provider integration is new" />
  <Stat value="Days" label="Integrator, per source" note="Depends entirely on how open the source system is" />
  <Stat value="Ongoing" label="Data steward" note="A few hours a month once conventions are set" />
</Stats>

The integration work is the variable that dominates. A source system with an open API is a
day; one that only exports CSV on request is a project of its own. Establish this early,
it is the single biggest driver of how long a first project takes. Increasingly, some of
this work is done by agents rather than people.
[Agents that build and run integrations →](/value/ai-agents#agents-that-build-and-run-your-integrations)

## When you need outside help

You do not need a supplier to run DataHub, and you should not need one to build your model.
Outside help is genuinely useful for two things:

- **Connecting difficult source systems**, particularly legacy control and historian
  systems with proprietary interfaces.
- **Facilitating the first modelling sessions**, if your organisation has never done
  structured modelling before. <span className="t-brand">The facilitator should be teaching
  your experts to do it, not doing it for them.</span>

If a proposal has a supplier building your model and handing it over, that is the pattern
described above, and it tends to end the same way.

## Where agents fit in this table

An agent is not a new role in the chart. It attaches to the roles already there: drafting what
an integrator would write, assembling what an analyst would assemble, watching what an
operator cannot watch continuously.

The line that does not move is accountability. Approving work, accepting a mapping, signing a
figure and owning the naming conventions stay with the named person, and the agent's
contribution is that they arrive at that decision with the evidence already gathered.
