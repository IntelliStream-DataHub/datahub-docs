# Flow clients and secrets

<Personas>
  <Persona primary>Administrators</Persona>
  <Persona>IT security</Persona>
</Personas>

<KeyIdea>
An organisation keeps two kinds of credential for its flows and [agents](/using/agents).
A **registered client** is a Keycloak client that runs act as instead of the person who
deployed them, so <span className="hl">a flow keeps running whoever built it</span>. A
**secret** is a named value, such as a password, that a flow refers to without the value
ever appearing in its definition. Both belong to the organisation, which manages them itself,
and both are stored in Vault.
</KeyIdea>

## Who may change them

| What | Group in the organisation |
| --- | --- |
| See the names of the clients and secrets | `/settings/flow/read` |
| Register, replace or remove a client or a secret | `/settings/flow/write` |

Read and write are separate grants, and write does not imply read; `/settings/*/read` and
`/settings/*/write` cover every settings scope. These are settings groups, not the flow
groups: someone may build flows without managing their credentials, and the other way round.
The same groups cover the [tool servers](/administration/agents#tool-servers) agents may call.

Nobody can read a value back. A client's secret and a secret's value are written and never
shown again, by the console or the API.

## Registered clients {#registered-clients}

A flow deployed as a person runs on that person's offline session, which lapses after a while
without a run and ends with their account. A registered client has neither limit, so it suits
a flow or an agent that must keep running. [How long a deployment as a person
lasts →](/administration/agents#how-long-a-deployment-lasts)

<Steps>
  <Step title="Create the client in Keycloak">
    In the realm, a confidential client with **Client authentication** and **Service accounts
    roles** on. Give its service-account user the `DATAHUB_ACCESS` role, and copy the client
    secret from its **Credentials** tab.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Give it its access">
    Make the service-account user a member of your organisation, and put it in the groups
    its flows need, such as `/datasets/<id>/read` and `/datasets/<id>/write`. It needs write
    on the data set of every flow that runs as it, since that is where a failed run's event
    is written.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Register it">
    In the console, **Flows**, then **Clients**. Give it a short name, lowercase letters,
    digits and dashes, and paste its client id, as Keycloak shows it rather than its internal
    UUID, and its secret. The credentials are not tried until a flow is deployed to run as it.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Run a flow as it">
    On the flow or agent, **Run as a client**, and the name. Deploying checks that Keycloak
    accepts the client and that it may write the flow's data set.
  </Step>
</Steps>

**Assigning a client is limited to people who hold every group it holds.** Without that
rule, anyone allowed to deploy could give a flow more access than they have themselves. An
administrator, `DATAHUB_ADMIN`, may assign any client, and a client that is itself an
administrator can only be assigned by one.

**To rotate a client's secret**, regenerate it in Keycloak, then **Replace** it under
**Flows**, then **Clients**, with both the client id and the new secret: both are required
every time. Runs take the new secret at once. Until it is replaced, runs as that client fail,
and its flows show *needs re-authorisation*; once the stored secret works again, deploy them
again.

**Removing a client** removes only its registration. Flows that run as it fail until they are
deployed again, as a person or as another client, and the client stays in Keycloak: disable
it there, or rotate its secret, to end its access.

## Secrets {#secrets}

A secret is a value a flow needs but should not carry, such as a password for an outside
system. A step refers to it as `${secrets.<name>}`, and only in a property marked sensitive,
where the whole value must be that one reference. [References in a flow →](/using/flow-references)

The console has no page for secrets yet. They are stored and removed through the API's
tenant settings, under `/tenant/settings/flow/secrets/<name>`, by someone holding
`/settings/flow/write`. A run that refers to a secret the organisation does not have fails,
naming the secret.

## Where they are stored

In the organisation's own Vault secret, `tenant-config/<organisation name>`, beside its
model settings, its tool servers, and the offline tokens of the flows deployed as a person:

| Key | What it holds |
| --- | --- |
| `flow.client.<name>.client-id`, `flow.client.<name>.client-secret` | A registered client |
| `flow.secret.<name>` | A secret |
| `flow.token.<flow>` | A deployer's offline token, written by the flow service |

The API writes the clients and secrets; the flow service reads them when a run starts. **A
removed or replaced value stays in Vault's version history** until Vault rolls it out, so
removing a credential is no substitute for rotating it where it was issued.
